# Hamilton et al. 2021 - Key Numbers

**Paper:** "Disease-driven mass mortality event leads to widespread extirpation and variable recovery potential of a marine predator across the eastern Pacific"
**Source:** Proc. R. Soc. B 288: 20211195
**Dataset:** 48,810 surveys, 34 data contributors

## Range-wide Declines
- **Density declined 94.3% range-wide** (92.5% shallow, 96.5% deep)
- **Occurrence declined 52.3% range-wide** (64.1% shallow, 55.3% deep)

## Regional Shallow-Water Declines (density)
- **Baja California to Cape Flattery WA: >99.2% declines** (functionally extinct)
  - Baja California: no observations since 2015
  - Southern California: no observations since 2018  
  - Oregon: no observations since ~2017
  - Central California: >99.2%
  - Northern California: >99.2%
  - Washington outer coast: >99.2%
- **Northern regions: >87.8% declines**
  - Salish Sea: 92.4%
  - British Columbia: 87.9%
  - Southeast Alaska: 96.0%
  - East Gulf of Alaska: 93.8%

## Epidemic Timing (crash = 75% decline in occurrence)
- Baja California: crashed within **2.1 months** of outbreak
- Southern California: **6.3 months**
- Central California: **<2 years**
- Northern California, Oregon: **<3 years**
- Washington outer coast, Salish Sea, BC, SE Alaska: **~4 years**
- East GOA: **<3 years**
- West GOA/Aleutians: ~17 months (uncertain)

## Temperature Relationship
- Temperature importance in SDM rose **>4x** after outbreak (to ~40% permutation importance)
- Pre-outbreak: probability peaked at **~16°C**
- Post-outbreak: curve shifted to peak at **~5°C**, near 0% by 23°C

## Recovery (as of 2020)
- **No population recovery observed since 2017** in any region
- Southern regions (Baja to WA outer coast): "flat-lined" at near-zero
- Northern regions with remaining animals: **continued decline 2017-2020** (SE AK, BC, Salish Sea; p<0.001)
- SE Alaska: 60% of surveyed cells still had common observations
- Occurrence ≥32% in northern regions (Salish Sea and north)

## Key Insights for Model Calibration
1. Strong latitudinal gradient in crash speed AND severity
2. Warmer = faster crash AND fewer survivors
3. No recovery even 7 years post-outbreak (as of 2020)
4. Northern populations may STILL be declining (not stabilized)
5. Genetic resistance hypothesis: possible latitudinal gradient in resistance
6. Schiebelhut et al. suggests genetic underpinning for SSWD resistance
7. Captive breeding feasible but scaling is a challenge
8. Temperature drives speed/severity but doesn't explain trigger
